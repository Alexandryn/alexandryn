## Context

The forces at play. What constraint, requirement, or problem made this decision
necessary? What did we know at the time — and, just as importantly, what did we
not know?

Write this so that a reader in two years can judge whether the reasoning still
holds, without having to reconstruct the situation.

## Decision

What we are doing. Active voice, present tense: "We use X." Not "It was decided
that X might be used."

## Options considered

### Option A — <name>

Pros, cons, and why it was or wasn't chosen.

### Option B — <name>

Same.

Record the options that lost. The next person will think of them too, and
deserves to know they were already weighed.

## Consequences

**Good** — what this buys us.

**Bad** — what it costs us. Every real decision has a cost; if this section is
empty, the analysis is incomplete.

**Neutral** — what changes without clearly being better or worse.

## Reversal cost

How hard is this to undo later, and what would force the question open again?
A decision that's cheap to reverse deserves less deliberation than one that
isn't — say which this is.

## Confidence

How sure are we, honestly? If this was a judgement call made with incomplete
information, say so here. Constitution §12.
